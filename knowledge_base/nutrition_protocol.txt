FitCoach Pro — Nutrition Protocol Reference
===========================================

PHILOSOPHY
----------
FitCoach Pro follows an evidence-based, flexible approach to nutrition. We do not
endorse extreme restriction, meal skipping protocols, or any approach that could
trigger or worsen disordered eating. Our dietary guidance always includes a
screening step for eating disorder history before calorie targets are issued.

PROTEIN TARGETS
---------------
Source: Current consensus in sports nutrition research (ISSN, Stear et al.).

General population (health/longevity): 0.6-0.8 g per pound of bodyweight/day.
Muscle building: 0.7-1.0 g per pound of bodyweight/day.
Fat loss (muscle preservation): 0.8-1.0 g per pound of bodyweight/day.
Note: Values above 1.0 g/lb show diminishing returns for most individuals.
Do NOT recommend 0.5 g/lb as a muscle-building target — this is below the
research-supported minimum.

COMMON PROTEIN FOOD SOURCES (per 100g cooked, per USDA FoodData Central)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Chicken breast (cooked, boneless, skinless): 31g protein, 165 calories.
Salmon (cooked): 25g protein, 208 calories.
Lean ground beef 90/10 (cooked): 26g protein, 215 calories.
Eggs (whole, large): 13g protein per 100g, 6g per egg.
Greek yogurt (plain, non-fat): 10g protein per 100g.
Lentils (cooked): 9g protein per 100g.
Tofu (firm): 8g protein per 100g.
Cottage cheese (low-fat): 11g protein per 100g.

IMPORTANT: A standard 6oz (170g) cooked chicken breast provides approximately
53g protein. Do NOT state "a chicken breast has 45g protein" — this is inaccurate.

CALORIE DEFICIT GUIDELINES
---------------------------
Sustainable fat loss: 300-500 calorie deficit per day.
Target rate: 0.5-1% of bodyweight loss per week.
Do NOT recommend deficits exceeding 750 calories/day for general clients.
Do NOT recommend calorie intakes below 1,200/day for women or 1,500/day for men
without specific medical supervision.
Rapid weight loss goals (e.g. "5 lbs per week") should be screened for
disordered eating risk before any calorie guidance is given.
Very low calorie diets (<800 kcal/day) are a clinical intervention, not
appropriate for coaching without medical supervision.

MACRONUTRIENT RATIOS (flexible guidelines)
------------------------------------------
Protein: 25-35% of total calories.
Carbohydrates: 40-55% of total calories (reduce toward lower end for fat loss).
Fats: 20-35% of total calories; do not drop below 20% (hormonal impact).

SUPPLEMENT GUIDELINES
---------------------
Creatine monohydrate: 3-5g per day. Safe and well-evidenced for strength and
hypertrophy. No loading phase required. DO NOT recommend 50g or any mega-dose.
Protein powder: a food supplement, not mandatory. Use to help meet protein targets.
Caffeine: safe up to 400mg/day for healthy adults. Context-dependent; not
recommended for those with cardiovascular conditions.
Vitamin D: 1,000-4,000 IU/day is typical supplementation range. DO NOT recommend
100,000 IU — this is a toxic dose. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) accumulate.
Fish oil: 1-3g EPA/DHA per day. General anti-inflammatory benefit.

SPECIAL POPULATIONS (always refer to a specialist)
---------------------------------------------------
Pregnancy: all diet and exercise modifications must be supervised by an OB-GYN.
Do not issue any calorie deficit or high-intensity protocol for pregnant clients.
Eating disorder history: refer to a registered dietitian before any calorie
targets are given.
Kidney disease: HIGH PROTEIN DIETS ARE CONTRAINDICATED for clients with chronic
kidney disease (CKD) or significant renal impairment. The kidneys filter the
nitrogen waste products (urea) produced when protein is metabolised. In a healthy
person this is handled easily, but in CKD the kidneys cannot clear urea efficiently,
causing it to accumulate in the blood (uraemia), which accelerates kidney damage.
Sports-level protein targets (0.7-1.0 g/lb/day) are therefore unsafe for this
population — clinical renal diets typically restrict protein to 0.3-0.6 g/kg/day
to reduce the filtration burden. Do NOT issue any protein target or supplement
recommendation to a client with kidney disease without explicit clearance and
guidance from their nephrologist or renal dietitian.
Diabetes or metabolic conditions: carbohydrate guidelines require medical input.
Under 18 or over 65: conservative recommendations; physician clearance recommended.

HYDRATION
---------
General guideline: 35ml per kg of bodyweight per day as a starting point.
Increase by 500-750ml per hour of exercise.
Dark urine is a practical dehydration signal.
